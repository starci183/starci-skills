## Ownership test

Ask why the anatomy exists:

1. If it is product-neutral interaction or visual language—button, copy hierarchy, field, surface,
   collection, navigation, rail, workspace, spacing, responsive, focus, scroll, sticky, resize or
   overlay behavior—Grammar owns it.
2. If it exists because of StarCi business meaning—course facts, learning progress, daily quests,
   interview states, AI quota, checkout, leaderboard or repository evidence—the application owns a
   `block` or `composite`.
3. Every application block must bind each rendered role to published Grammar authority. Local business
   ordering is allowed; a local replacement for generic Grammar anatomy is not.

## Proposal approval gate

An agent may discover a `grammar-gap`, but may not silently solve it by publishing an export. It must
present the teacher with the exact proposal, including anatomy, universal invariants, at least two
independent consumers or one platform invariant, responsive/focus/scroll behavior, alternatives,
migration boundary and risks. Until explicit approval, the proposal remains non-renderable and
non-importable. Approval applies only to the described anatomy; widening it requires approval again.

## Examples

- `ContextIntro` is Grammar: its eyebrow-heading-description hierarchy is product-neutral.
- `CourseCard` is an application block: course media, price and learning progress are business facts;
  it composes Grammar surface, media, copy and action authorities.
- `ChatWorkspace` is Grammar only for pane, scroll, focus and responsive ownership. Provider state,
  message policy, quota and assistant behavior remain application-owned.
- `DashboardShell` is Grammar only for shell regions and responsive transformation. Metrics, quests,
  streaks and recommendations remain application blocks.

## Fail-closed result

A missing generic authority produces `grammar-gap`. A business-specific structure without complete
Grammar bindings produces `application-composition-gap`. Neither permits local utility imitation.
