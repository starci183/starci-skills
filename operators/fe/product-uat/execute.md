# Execute `fe/product-uat`

## Step 1 — Bind the approved journey

**Read:** business outcome, approved flow, passed fidelity receipt, and optional prior UX/UI resolution. **Context:** UAT evaluates user outcome, not visual similarity; a closure rerun freezes the exact request and repair-receipt identities. **Session write:** journey checklist and deterministic identity. **Stop:** if fidelity has not passed or the prior request set is stale.

## Step 2 — Exercise the complete task

**Read:** running app, deterministic identity, approved flow/detail/container anatomy, and exact proof knowledge. **Context:** orchestration may parallelize independent roles but not sequential journey state. For every user-visible claim, trace the runtime payload to the rendered DOM and assert meaningful content, not merely that a container, heading, control, label, or status exists. Bind each visible region to its exact Grammar object identity and verify that object's required anatomy: surface owner, repeated-row owner, separators, state markers, labels, and interaction. For every approved page, modal, drawer, popover, or inline decision, exercise its trigger and prove actual container identity, background behavior, dismissal/back path, focus return, scroll ownership, and mobile transformation. Surface count or visual similarity cannot substitute for object or container identity. Exercise required entitlement and populated/empty variants. A control may become actionable only when its prerequisite content and state are present; explicitly test the negative path. **Session write:** entry, progress, navigation, confirmation, result, failure, retry, resume, Grammar-object identity, semantic-content, state-marker mapping, interaction-container fidelity, and prerequisite-control evidence when present in the flow. **Stop:** when any required step or semantic assertion is untested, when only an expired/empty fixture was exercised, when a surface claims content that its body does not contain, or when the chosen Grammar object, state mapping, or interaction container is not proven in both DOM anatomy and a rendered viewport.

## Step 3 — Route failures and potentials

**Read:** UAT evidence only. **Context:** classify every hard issue as `ux-ui`, `functional`, `backend-contract`, or `business`, then assign one owner capability. Source is evidence, never automatic design authority. A visual shell with missing semantic content is a failure even when screenshots, lint, and component existence checks pass. A hard UX/UI issue emits `ux-ui-repair`; generic repair is invalid. A passing rerun with prior request identities emits closure routing. **Session write:** outcome, issues, semantic checks, potential signals, exact resolution request refs, and state. **Stop:** before applying repairs, closing requests, or changing business authority. **Orchestration:** independent lenses may challenge state semantics, hierarchy, Grammar identity, interaction, and content; the coordinator owns classification.
