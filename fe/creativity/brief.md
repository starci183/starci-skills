# design brief

## Definition

The design brief is the smallest complete statement of what the page is for. It fixes the user,
their current situation, the page thesis, the next valuable action, required evidence and states
before visual directions compete.

The deciding question: **if every decorative choice disappeared, would the brief still explain why
this page deserves to exist?**

## Rules

**BRIEF-1 · State one page thesis.**

Complete this sentence: “This page helps [user in situation] achieve [outcome] by making [decisive
information or action] easiest to find.” A page with two theses is two pages or one page without a
priority.

**BRIEF-2 · Name one primary action and its earned moment.**

The primary action follows the product's core loop and appears where motivation and ability meet.
Secondary paths are named separately so they cannot acquire equal visual weight by accident.

**BRIEF-3 · Turn product facts into an information order.**

List what must be understood first, what supports a decision, what can wait and what is optional.
This order becomes block hierarchy; it is not a list of desired components.

**BRIEF-4 · Declare states and anti-goals.**

Write which situations the page must handle and what it must not become. Anti-goals protect the
design from attractive but irrelevant additions during divergence.

**BRIEF-5 · Expose assumptions.**

Anything not established by the user, source or data is an assumption. Mark it, state how it affects
the result and prefer the version easiest to reverse.

## Forbidden

| Never | Why it is refused | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Describe the page as a collection of components | Components do not explain the user's outcome | State a user, situation, outcome and decision |
| List several primary actions | Equal primaries split attention | Choose one and demote the rest |
| Hide assumptions inside copy or fixtures | They will be mistaken for product truth | Mark assumptions explicitly |
| Omit anti-goals | Scope grows through individually plausible additions | Name what the page must not optimize for |

## Examples

### A page thesis

```
help a returning learner resume the highest-value next activity without first interpreting every metric
```

```
show courses, streak, goals, rewards, challenges and news in a modern dashboard
```

They differ in one thing: whether the brief names a user outcome or an inventory.
