# design divergence

## Definition

Design divergence creates several materially different answers to the same brief while keeping
product truth and canon fixed. It explores hierarchy and behavior, not arbitrary visual variation.

The deciding question: **would choosing another direction change what the user notices, understands
or does first?**

## Rules

**DIVERGENCE-1 · Produce two to four directions before selecting one.**

One direction is the first answer wearing the authority of a decision, and the first answer is
rarely challenged once it is the only one drawn. More than four stops being a choice and becomes an
idea catalogue: selection turns into browsing, and the reason each direction existed is lost by the
time the last one is read.

The usual shape is a conservative direction that minimizes change, a balanced direction that makes
one strong product bet, and a bold direction that reorganizes the experience around the page thesis.
Take two when the scope genuinely holds only one real bet, and a fourth only when a decision model
exists that none of the other three tests. In migration and mixed work one of them is the
parity-first direction, because a redesign with nothing to compare against cannot be reviewed as
either parity or proposal.

**DIVERGENCE-2 · Vary structural axes, not canon.**

Directions may vary information hierarchy, CTA moment, progressive disclosure, density, responsive
reordering and which blocks are peers. They may not vary tier boundaries, token ladders, icon policy,
loading semantics or vendor ownership.

**DIVERGENCE-3 · Give every direction a causal sentence.**

State: “Because [evidence], this direction makes [thing] lead, so the user can [outcome].” If the
sentence merely describes appearance, the direction has no product hypothesis.

**DIVERGENCE-4 · Sketch with known contracts.**

Represent each direction as a page/block/contract graph using the current inventory. Mark a genuine
grammar gap as a proposal; do not make the sketch appear legal with anonymous boxes.

**DIVERGENCE-5 · Preserve the state matrix across directions.**

A direction is not stronger because it ignores empty, loading or failure. Each concept must explain
how the same user journey survives those states.

## Forbidden

| Never | Why it is refused | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Three versions differing only by colour, radius or icon | They test no different product idea | Change hierarchy, sequencing or disclosure |
| Let the bold direction ignore canon | Illegality is not creativity | Make a bolder product bet within the grammar |
| Hide a missing contract behind “custom section” | The design cannot be implemented honestly | Mark and justify the proposed grammar addition |
| Select while generating | Later ideas become arguments for the first one | Finish all directions before critique |

## Examples

### Materially different directions

```
continuity-led: resume work leads; goals explain what follows
progress-led: goal deficit leads; next work is derived from it
deadline-led: expiring commitments lead; continuity becomes supporting context
```

```
version A: white card
version B: tinted card
version C: card with an icon tile
```

They differ in one thing: whether the user encounters another decision model.
