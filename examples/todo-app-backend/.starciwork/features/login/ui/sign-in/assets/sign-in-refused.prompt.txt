Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: proposed visual direction for the Todo web product; representative sign-in / refused state; desktop-1280
Primary request: Render a polished, implementation-ready desktop interface direction for a sign-in page after a credential pair has been refused. This is a design direction, not a browser screenshot and not proof of a running product.

Scene/backdrop: Full-height application canvas in very light neutral grey. No navigation, sidebar, footer, browser chrome, device frame, illustration, photograph, logo, mascot, or decorative art. Centre one compact sign-in surface vertically and horizontally with calm surrounding whitespace.

Brand: Use Inter. Use white (#FFFFFF) for the bounded surface and #2F6BFF only for the primary action and restrained focus/accent details. All normal text on white must read at strong contrast; never place low-contrast text on #2F6BFF. No unapproved saturated hues.

Grammar anatomy: Follow the installed @starci/grammar 0.4.13 Common family on HeroUI v3, based on its README, dist/common/index.d.ts, renderer types, renderer JavaScript, and dist/common/styles.css. Show the visual anatomy of PageContainer/formPageClassName, one top-level SurfaceCard at compact form measure, Heading level 1, Text, Input(kind email), Input(kind password), and Button(type submit, variant primary, width fill). The refused pair is a surface-level settled negative state; place one Text(live assertive) line near the fields, not a field-specific error and not a coloured custom alert. Preserve visible labels above both inputs. Keep the password empty after refusal and retain the email.

Composition and hierarchy: Surface class is form. One focal point in three seconds: “Sign in”. Inside the card use a clean left-aligned stack with a small heading-to-description gap, a larger gap before the form, consistent field rhythm, then the refusal sentence and the full-width primary button. The card should feel like an unmistakable sign-in page, not a generic card sample. Exactly one filled accent action.

Text (verbatim):
- “Sign in”
- “Welcome back. Sign in to continue.”
- “Email”
- “alex@example.com”
- “Password”
- “We couldn’t sign you in with that email and password.”
- “Sign in”

Knowledge constraints: Apply LAYOUT-1, HIERARCHY-1 through HIERARCHY-3, CTA-1 through CTA-3, ACTION-1, STATE-1, STATE-2, FEEDBACK-3, ACCENT-1, RESPONSIVE-1, COVERAGE-1, GAP-3/GAP-4/GAP-5, PADDING-4, MEASURE-1/MEASURE-4, SURFACE-2, BOUNDARY-6, FONT-2/FONT-3/FONT-4, TONE-1/TONE-2, FLOW-1/FLOW-2, A11Y-1/A11Y-4, BRAND-1/BRAND-3, COLOR-5, TRUTH-4, and TASTE-1 through TASTE-7/TASTE-9/TASTE-10.

Responsive intent: At mobile-390 the same order remains, the card becomes fluid within the page inset, labels wrap naturally, and no control is hidden or reordered.

Avoid: no existing frontend implementation as a source, no second card, no split-screen hero, no social sign-in, no forgot-password link, no sign-up link, no extra checkbox, no red banner, no invented icon, no gradients, no glassmorphism, no excessive shadows, no tiny labels, no watermark, no extra copy, no misspelled text.
