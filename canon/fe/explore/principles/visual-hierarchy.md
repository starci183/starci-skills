# Visual hierarchy

Generalises two existing rules — one primary action per screen, and the quiet eyebrow — into a single
statement: rank is the job of TYPOGRAPHY and COLOUR, not of adding another frame or box.

## The rule of thumb

**At most one MAIN action per screen; rank between elements comes from SIZE, WEIGHT and COLOUR
inside one system — never from adding a border, a background or a new box to "emphasise" something.**

## The rules

**One `primary` (solid accent) per surface.** Every other action drops to `secondary`, `tertiary` or
`ghost`. A second solid button beside the first is a second voice at equal volume: the hierarchy is
gone and the reader cannot tell what to press first.

**A secondary label or identifier** — an ordinal like "Thử thách N", an eyebrow, meta or a count —
always ranks BELOW the title: smaller and dimmer (`text-xs text-muted`), never larger, bolder or more
saturated than the title it belongs to. An inverted rank, where the secondary outshouts the primary,
is a defect to fix rather than a successful emphasis.

**Rank within a group of text is size, weight and colour token** — not a chip or a border added
around the part you want noticed. Jewellery that does not correspond to real rank is read as noise.

**Accent is one of the tools of hierarchy, not all of them.** Use it only for the four roles
[[accent-system]] defines; do not paint something accent-coloured simply to emphasise it.

## Already applied

The CTA rule (primary = solid + size lg + arrow, at most one primary per surface); the challenge
section's secondary label kept as a quiet eyebrow rather than promoted to a heading;
[[accent-system]] §1 limiting accent to four roles, of which the CTA is one.
